01 · the problem

Carbon markets are a black box.

A $900bn industry running on PDFs and proprietary registries (UNDP, 2022).

01

Double-counting

The same offset gets sold to two buyers by two different registries. Nobody catches it because no one can see the full ledger.

02

Retirement fraud

Credits "retired" by one firm quietly reappear on another firm's ESG report six months later.

03

Zero auditability

Want to verify a claim? Email a PDF. Wait three weeks. Pray the registry still exists.

"More than 90% of rainforest carbon offsets by biggest certifier are worthless" — Greenfield, The Guardian, 2023
rajdeepchaudhari · 2026

The voluntary carbon market moved roughly nine hundred billion dollars last year. That number matters. What's broken is that despite the size, the underlying record-keeping is still email, spreadsheets, and proprietary registries you have to request access to.

Three concrete failure modes: double-counting, where the same credit gets sold twice; retirement fraud, where burned credits quietly come back; and zero auditability, because you can't verify anything without going through the registry.

The Guardian investigation in 2023 showed more than ninety percent of rainforest offsets from the world's biggest certifier weren't real. That's not a Verra problem. It's a ledger problem. When the record is private, all three failures become invisible.

Greenify solves the ledger part. Not MRV, not verification methodology, just the data layer.

rajdeepchaudhari · 2026

Here's the full system. Three tiers, each with one job.

The client is a React + Vite single-page app. The user talks to MetaMask; the SPA builds transactions with ethers and gets them signed.

The edge is a set of Vercel serverless functions. They pin project metadata to IPFS through Pinata, and they expose a REST API for the frontend.

The state tier has two pieces. Ethereum Sepolia holds the canonical state. MongoDB caches decoded events so the frontend can load fast without hitting the RPC every time.

The important property is the arrow direction: events flow from the chain to Mongo, never the other way. If I wipe the Mongo database, the next sync rebuilds the whole state from the chain. No off-chain service ever writes truth.

Technology choices. I deliberately went boring.

For the contracts, I used OpenZeppelin v5 for everything that could be reused. AccessControl for roles, ERC-1155 for the token, ReentrancyGuard for the marketplace. I didn't reimplement any of these. They're audited by people who do this full-time.

Frontend is React + Vite + TypeScript + ethers v6. Standard stack. I chose Vite over Next because I don't need SSR, and the dev loop is faster.

For the backend, originally I had an Express server, but free-tier hosting kept putting it to sleep and dropping my WebSocket subscriptions to the RPC. I moved everything to Vercel serverless functions and reworked the indexer to use a polling checkpoint instead. One deploy now ships both the SPA and the API.

MongoDB Atlas free tier for the event cache. Pinata for IPFS. Everything fits inside free plans, which matters for a coursework project.

03 · smart contracts

Three contracts, one responsibility each.

1

ProjectRegistry

Who can register a project and who can approve it.

  • registerProject(cid) — permissionless
  • approveProject(id) — VERIFIER_ROLE
  • recordIssuance(id, amt) — called by credit
2

CarbonCredit (ERC-1155)

Token: tokenId == projectId, 1 unit = 1 tCO₂e.

  • mint(to, id, amt) — MINTER_ROLE
  • retire(id, amt) — burns + emits receipt
  • Uses Registry approval check before mint
3

Marketplace

Escrowed listing book with capped protocol fee.

  • list(id, amt, price) — escrows credits
  • buy(id, amt) — payable, checks-effects-interactions
  • Fee hard-capped at 10%, ReentrancyGuard

Why ERC-1155? ERC-20 loses per-project provenance; ERC-721 over-models (credits are fungible inside a project). ERC-1155 is the right shape (Radomski et al., 2018).

rajdeepchaudhari · 2026

Three contracts. Each does one thing.

ProjectRegistry is the list of environmental projects. Anyone can register, but only a verifier role can approve. In a production deployment the verifier would be a Gnosis Safe multisig; for the coursework it's a single test account with the role granted.

CarbonCredit is the ERC-1155 token. I chose ERC-1155 deliberately. ERC-20 would collapse every credit into one pool and you'd lose the ability to prove a retirement came from a specific project. ERC-721 treats each credit as a unique NFT, which is overkill because credits inside a project really are fungible. ERC-1155 sits in the middle: one contract, many project batches, each fungible internally. The key function is retire, which burns the credits and emits the receipt event.

Marketplace is the listing book. Sellers escrow credits into the contract; buyers pay with ETH. I follow checks-effects-interactions and apply ReentrancyGuard as belt-and-braces, cap the protocol fee at 10% in two places, and use custom errors everywhere.

All three are verified on Etherscan.

    Sepolia mines

    e.g. ProjectRegistered(id, owner, cid)

rajdeepchaudhari · 2026

This is the end-to-end lifecycle of any write action in the app.

Step 1, the SPA builds the transaction. ethers v6 gives me typed contract bindings from the ABI, so there's no string-concatenation of function signatures.

Step 2, MetaMask pops up and asks the user to sign. The private key never leaves the browser extension; the signed hex comes back to me.

Step 3, I forward that to Sepolia via a public RPC. While it's mining, my TxBanner component shows a yellow pending state with the hash linked to Etherscan.

Step 4, the contract emits an event on success. This is the crucial part: events are how the off-chain world finds out something happened.

Step 5, my /api/sync serverless function polls every minute. It reads lastBlock from Mongo, queries logs in chunked ranges, and writes each event.

Step 6, upserts mean the whole thing is idempotent. Running sync twice on the same range does nothing the second time. That's the property that lets me wipe the DB and rebuild from scratch without fear.

Wrapping up with honest limitations and references.

The biggest limitation is the single-account verifier. Whoever holds that role can approve fraudulent projects. In production, role rotation to a multisig plus a formal MRV pipeline is non-negotiable. For this coursework, it's just me.

The Vercel free tier caps cron jobs at daily intervals. For sub-hour sync during a demo I use cron-job.org as a free secondary trigger. A paid plan or self-hosted scheduler is the clean answer long-term.
